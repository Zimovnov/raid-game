# berezka — camouflage art brief

**Reference status:** see [`sources.json`](/Users/vladimir/Downloads/RAID_GAME_Art_Overhaul_References/camouflage/berezka/sources.json). Five visually checked references cover full suits, a reverse garment view and a mesh/print close-up. The pattern is historical: the Russian Armed Forces Museum describes Berezka as a Soviet border-troop camouflage developed in the 1970s ([museum exhibition](https://museum-vf.ru/exhibition/iskusstvo-maskirovki-obraztsy-kamuflyazha-sssr-1968-1991-gg/)). No contemporary issue claim is made. Photos are reference-only, and the runtime material must be independently authored.

## Target print and material

Create an original light woodland family with a subdued sage/gray-green ground, broken by irregular pale silver-gray leaf or short vertical brush marks and sparse darker green interruptions. Keep the marks asymmetrical, unevenly spaced, and softly edged; no regular vertical ladder, exact silhouettes, density map, or exact Soviet print topology. The target is a readable low-poly game material, not a reproduction.

The macro reference is KZS mesh and must only guide how open weave and print edges can read together; do not apply that open mesh treatment to every cloth variant. For ordinary woven garments use matte, fine fabric response. Keep camouflage as a fabric-only change to the selected apparel cut; do not invent armor or load-bearing construction.

## Scale, UV and motion

Use small-to-medium leaf marks that remain distinct on a torso and trouser leg, with enough larger pale breaks to preserve silhouette disruption at gameplay distance. Do not mirror UVs or align repeated marks across shoulders, cuffs, seat, knees, pocket flaps, or seams. Check close crop, whole garment, and normal gameplay distance through standing, shoulder raise, crouch, kneel, elbow and knee bends. Keep mark scale stable across LOD and avoid moiré or marks stretching into thin streaks.
