## Pattern and material

Create a coherent original green digital woodland candidate: a shaded olive/forest-green ground, grouped dark pine/near-black blocks and small muted earth/tan transitions. Avoid bright pale base dominance, because the pale swatch in the reference set is only a pixel/textile-scale analogy. Build a three-scale hierarchy: broad mottled fields, connected medium digital islands, and sparse small square-step fragments. Keep pixel edges visible at close range, but soften the overall frequencies with cluster grouping so distance silhouettes do not turn into LCD noise. Do not copy any national pixel pattern, named camouflage, supplier swatch or exact repeat from the sources.

Use a matte ripstop field-cloth response. The sampled folded textiles establish weave scale and drape; the worn BDU examples show that the print should remain legible across chest, sleeves, seat and legs. Keep folds and seam shadows in garment shading, not painted as fake geometry. The camouflage variant changes fabric only; use the selected jacket/trousers cut from its apparel brief, with no new armor or load-bearing design.

## Scale, UV and pose review

Pixel-cell scale must be visible close, grouped pattern readable at distance; ensure UV joins do not create repeated checker seams. Make separate panel UVs follow the fabric direction where directional, but do not mirror or reuse a recognizable cluster. Continue color density across shoulder, cuff, crotch, knee and pocket seams. In crouch, kneel, shoulder raise and first-person sleeve bends, the pattern must deform with fabric rather than smear, bunch into a hard seam, or disappear under filtering. Review a close crop, a full garment, and the normal gameplay distance; keep LOD pattern frequency stable and avoid moiré.
